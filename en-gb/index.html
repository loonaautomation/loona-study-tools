What Are Study Tools — and Why Do They Work?

Study tools are small, focused helpers that turn passive reading into active learning. Research on learning keeps pointing to the same few techniques: retrieval practice (testing yourself instead of re-reading), spaced repetition (reviewing at growing intervals), deliberate practice (fresh questions at the right difficulty) and planning (a timetable you can actually see). Each tool on this page is built around one of those techniques.

Flashcards are the classic retrieval-practice tool: you see a question, try to recall the answer, then flip the card. Our Flashcards Maker adds shuffle mode and know / don't-know tracking, so a deck quickly sorts itself into what you have mastered and what still needs work.

Quizzes do the same job for a whole class. A teacher can build a quiz in minutes — multiple choice, true/false and short-answer questions — let students attempt it on any device with instant scoring, or print the quiz and a separate answer key for a paper test.

Worksheets give deliberate practice without the photocopier queue. Pick an operation, a number range and how many questions you want, and the generator creates a fresh, randomised worksheet — plus its answer key — every single time.

Reading speed matters because slow, effortful reading leaves less mental energy for understanding. Our Reading Speed Test measures your real speed on a full passage and then checks comprehension, because speed without understanding is just skimming.

Practice drills cover the daily basics: times tables with timed rounds, a spelling test that reads words aloud, and handwriting sheets for letters, numbers and your own word list. Around exam time, the Exam Countdown Planner spreads your subjects across the exact days remaining. For the classroom, teachers also get a Word Search Generator, a Matching Worksheet Generator and a fair Random Name Picker.

A timetable turns good intentions into a plan. Tell the planner your subjects, how many hours a day you honestly have, and your exam date, and it builds a balanced weekly grid that rotates subjects fairly — editable before you print it and pin it above your desk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Loona Study Tools really free?

Yes — every tool on this page is completely free, with no sign-up, no account and no watermark. They are supported by unobtrusive advertising so students and teachers never have to pay.

Do I need to install anything or create an account?

No. Every tool runs directly in your web browser on your phone, tablet or computer. There is nothing to download and no account to create.

Is my work private? Where are my flashcards and quizzes stored?

Your decks, quizzes and timetables are saved only in your own browser's local storage, on your own device. Nothing you type is uploaded to a server. You can export your work as files (CSV or JSON) to keep a backup or move it to another device.

I am a teacher. Can I print worksheets, quizzes and flashcards?

Yes. The Math Worksheet Generator prints a clean worksheet plus a separate answer key. The Quiz Maker prints the quiz and a separate answer key. Flashcards have a print mode that lays cards out on paper, ready to cut out.

Is there a typing test or a Pomodoro timer here?

To keep every tool in one home, the Typing Test lives on Loona Text Tools and time tools such as the time card and hours calculators live on Loona Hours. Links to both are in the footer of every page, and the GPA/CGPA calculators live on Loona Calculators.

Do the tools work offline?

Once a page has loaded, the tools themselves work fully offline because all the logic runs on your device. Your saved decks and quizzes also stay available in your browser.
