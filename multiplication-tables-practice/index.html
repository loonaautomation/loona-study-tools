Multiplication Tables Practice — Drill, Check, Master

In short: choose which times tables to drill, answer a round of questions, and get instant marking that shows exactly which table still needs work. A printable 12 × 12 multiplication chart is included below the practice tool.

Times tables are the arithmetic bedrock: fractions, long division and algebra all slow down when a child must work out 7 × 8 from scratch. Short, focused drill rounds — with instant feedback instead of a marked page tomorrow — are how tables become automatic.

🔒 Private by design: practice rounds run entirely on this device; no scores are stored or sent anywhere.

How to Use It

  1. Tick the tables you want to practise (start with one or two new tables plus one old one).
  2. Start a round and type each answer. Work as fast as you can without guessing.
  3. Check answers: every question is marked instantly, wrong answers show the correct product, and the summary names your weak table.
  4. Repeat daily: five minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Use the chart below to revise, or print it for the wall.

How It Works

Questions are generated randomly from your chosen tables (multipliers 2–12), so no two rounds repeat. Marking is exact and instant, and the end-of-round summary counts mistakes per table, turning a vague "practise your tables" into a precise instruction like "drill the 7s". The chart is a standard 12 × 12 product grid.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tables should a child learn first?

The usual order is 2, 5 and 10 (patterns are easy), then 3 and 4, then 6 and 9 (which have helpful tricks), then 7, 8, 11 and 12. This tool lets you practise any mix, so old tables stay fresh while new ones are learned.

How long should practice sessions be?

Short and daily: 5–10 minutes. Automatic recall comes from many small repetitions spread over days, not from one long session.

Why does the summary name a "weak table"?

Because not all mistakes are equal. If three of your four errors were in the 7s, the fastest progress comes from drilling the 7s specifically — the summary counts errors per table so you know where to aim.

Is the multiplication chart printable?

Yes — the 12 × 12 chart on this page prints cleanly with the page. It is the classic chart used in classrooms worldwide.
