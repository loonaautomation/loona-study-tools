Loona Reaction Time Test — How Fast Are You?

In short: click the big box, wait for it to turn green, then click again as fast as you can. After five attempts you get your average reaction time in milliseconds, your best single attempt, and an honest guide to what your score means.

Reaction time is how quickly you respond to something you see. This free test measures yours properly: the wait is random every time, so you cannot guess, and a click that comes too early never counts. One round is five attempts — because a single click can be luck, but an average of five is a real measure.

An Honest Note About Accuracy

Phone touch screens, computer monitors and mice all add a small delay of their own — often tens of milliseconds — between the moment the colour changes and the moment your tap or click is registered. That delay is included in your score. So treat this test as a fair way to compare yourself with yourself over time, and to challenge friends on the same device — not as a laboratory measurement. The score bands below are indicative only, for the same reason.

How It Works

  1. Click to start, then wait. The box turns red and says “Wait for green…”. The wait is random — between 1.5 and 5 seconds — so nobody can guess it.
  2. Click the instant it turns green. The clock starts only when the green can actually be painted on your screen, and stops the moment your click or tap lands. Clicking too soon restarts that attempt and never counts against you.
  3. Repeat for five attempts. Your score is the average of the five, shown with your best single attempt and an indicative band — then saved to your on-device history so you can watch yourself improve.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Loona Reaction Time Test free?

Yes — completely free, with no sign-up and no download. It runs 100% in your browser, like every Loona Study Tool.

Is it accurate?

The timing itself is precise: the clock uses your browser's high-resolution timer and starts only once the green stimulus can be painted. But your device adds its own delay — touch screens and displays often add tens of milliseconds — so your score includes your device. Use it to compare with yourself, and with friends on the same device, rather than as a lab measurement.

Why five attempts?

One click can be a lucky guess or a single slip. Averaging five attempts cancels out most of that luck and gives a score that actually describes you. Clicking too soon never counts — that attempt simply restarts.

Does it save my scores?

Only on this device, in this browser's local storage. Your history, personal best and past rounds never leave your phone or computer, and there is no account. Use the “Clear history” button any time to erase them.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes — the test is built mobile-first and works with taps exactly as with mouse clicks. Just remember that phone touch screens usually add a little more latency than a mouse, so compare phone scores with phone scores.
